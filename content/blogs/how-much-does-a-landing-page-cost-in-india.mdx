Landing page prices vary enough that any single number can mislead. This post explains what actually moves the price, shares the published market ranges I could find with their limits, and shows how to compare quotes.

## What changes the price

- **Is the design already done?** Design plus development costs more than building from a finished design file.
- **How many sections and how much custom work.** A simple one-screen page differs from a long page with animation and interactive parts.
- **Copywriting.** Is the text supplied, or written for you?
- **Forms, tracking and integrations.** Connecting a CRM, email tool or ad conversion tracking adds work.
- **Speed and accessibility targets.** Hitting a strict mobile performance budget takes more care than a basic build.
- **Revisions and deadline.** Unlimited revisions and rush delivery both raise the price.

## Published market ranges (indicative only)

I looked at what freelancers publish for "high converting landing page design" on [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/services/71891-High-Converting-Landing-Page-Design). Listings ran from about $300 to $2,500, with most mid-range offers around $600 to $1,800. Many of those are **design only**, with development not included, so they are not comparable to a built, live page.

For hourly rates, one aggregator puts the median freelance rate in India at about $30 per hour, with the middle half between $15 and $50 ([source](https://www.secondtalent.com/cost-to-hire/india/)). These figures come from third-party pages with unclear methods, so treat them as a rough picture of the market, not a price list.

## How to compare quotes

1. Ask whether **design and development** are both included.
2. Ask what you own at the end: code, design files, domain and hosting access.
3. Ask how they will test **mobile speed**, and what number they aim for.
4. Ask what is not covered: copy, images, integrations, post-launch fixes.
5. Prefer a written scope with a fixed fee over a vague hourly estimate.
